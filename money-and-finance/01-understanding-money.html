🌍 Where Will You Use This?

Budgeting helps households, students, businesses and governments decide how to use limited money. The same mathematics appears in bank statements, payslips, invoices and personal financial planning.

🤔 Think About This...

You receive US$40. You need US$12 for transport, US$8 for lunch and want to save US$10.

How much money remains after meeting those plans?

US$40 − US$12 − US$8 − US$10 = US$10.

Money Has a Flow

Income Money received
Expenditure Money spent
Balance Money remaining
Key idea: A budget is a plan showing expected income, expected expenditure and the resulting balance.
Balance = Total income − Total expenditure

Types of Income

Earned income

Salary, wages, commission or payment for work.

Other income

Interest, rent received, gifts or business profit.

Fixed and Variable Expenses

Fixed expenses

Usually stay the same for a period, such as rent or a subscription.

Variable expenses

Change with use or choice, such as food, electricity or entertainment.

Worked Example: Preparing a Budget

Tariro receives US$180 in a month. Her expenses are transport US$45, food US$60, phone data US$18 and savings US$25.

1

Total planned outflow = 45 + 60 + 18 + 25 = US$148.

2

Balance = 180 − 148 = US$32.

3

She has a surplus of US$32.

Surplus and Deficit

Income > Expenditure → Surplus
Income < Expenditure → Deficit

💼 Money Smart Tip

Savings should be treated as a planned part of the budget, not only whatever happens to remain at the end.

🔍 How Do I Recognise This in an Exam?

  • “Prepare a budget” → organise income and expenses.
  • “Find the balance” → income minus expenditure.
  • “Surplus” → positive amount left.
  • “Deficit” → spending exceeds income.
  • Look carefully for deposits, withdrawals and transaction signs.

⚠️ Common Mistakes

Mistake 1: Adding expenditure to income instead of subtracting it.

Mistake 2: Forgetting small expenses when finding totals.

Mistake 3: Calling a positive balance a deficit.

Practice Questions

Foundation
  1. Classify salary, groceries, rent and electricity as income or expenditure.
  2. Monthly income is US$320 and expenditure is US$278. Find the balance.
  3. A budget has income US$150 and expenses US$172. State the deficit.
Developing
  1. Nyasha receives US$260. She spends US$55 on transport, US$74 on food, US$22 on data and US$35 on household items. She saves US$40. Find her remaining balance.
  2. A bank account opens with US$125. It receives US$80, then payments of US$36.50 and US$42.75 are made. Find the closing balance.
Challenge
  1. A family wants to save 15% of its US$800 income. Its fixed expenses are US$420 and variable expenses are US$205. Can it meet the savings goal? Find the final amount remaining.

One-Minute Summary

  • Income is money received; expenditure is money spent.
  • A budget plans income, spending and saving.
  • Balance = income − expenditure.
  • A positive balance is a surplus; a negative one is a deficit.
