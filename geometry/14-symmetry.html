GEOMETRY · LESSON 14

Symmetry

Identify line and rotational symmetry in shapes.

⏱️ 22–30 min 📘 Developing
90° 180° θ

Learning Objectives

  • identify line symmetry;
  • identify rotational symmetry;
  • state order of rotational symmetry;
  • complete symmetric figures;
  • recognise symmetry in regular polygons.

Introduction

This lesson develops Symmetry from meaning and patterns before moving to calculations. Look for what the quantities, shapes or symbols represent, then connect that idea to the method.

Key Notes

Read the definitions and key facts below carefully. Each rule is most useful when you can explain why it fits the situation, not simply recall it.

Line Symmetry

A line of symmetry divides a figure into two mirror-image halves.

Rotational Symmetry

A figure has rotational symmetry if it matches itself during a turn of less than 360°.

Order of rotational symmetry = number of matching positions in one full turn.

Common Examples

Square

4 lines; rotational order 4.

Rectangle

2 lines; rotational order 2.

Equilateral triangle

3 lines; rotational order 3.

Regular pentagon

5 lines; rotational order 5.

Try It: Rotational Symmetry

For a regular polygon, the order of rotational symmetry equals the number of sides.

🧠 Let’s Think Together

Worked Examples

Reason through the method

  1. Identify the known information and what must be found.
  2. Choose the definition, property or formula that connects them.
  3. Substitute carefully and show each step.
  4. Check the notation, units and whether the result is sensible.

Practice Questions

  1. How many lines of symmetry does a square have?
  2. State the rotational order of a rectangle.
  3. How many lines of symmetry does a regular hexagon have?
  4. Does a scalene triangle have line symmetry?
  5. State the rotational order of a regular octagon.

Common Mistakes

  • Choosing a rule before identifying what the question describes.
  • Skipping working or changing notation part-way through a solution.
  • Accepting an answer without checking its size, sign or units.

Exam Focus

Show the mathematical relationship you are using, keep notation consistent and give a clear final answer. Use a quick estimate, diagram or inverse operation to check that the result is reasonable.

Summary

  • Line symmetry creates mirror halves.
  • Rotational order counts matching positions in 360°.
