GEOMETRY · LESSON 01

Types of Angles

Recognise, name and classify angles accurately.

⏱️ 18–24 min 📘 Foundation
90° 180° θ

Learning Objectives

  • identify acute, right, obtuse, straight and reflex angles;
  • name angles using three letters;
  • measure and draw angles accurately;
  • estimate angle size before measuring;
  • recognise full turns and quarter turns.

Introduction

An angle measures the amount of turning between two rays that meet at a common point called the vertex.

Think about opening a door, turning a bicycle handlebar or moving the hands of a clock. In each case, the size of the turn can be described by an angle.

Angles are measured in degrees, written using the symbol °.

Key Notes

Acute

Greater than 0° but less than 90°.

Right

Exactly 90°.

Obtuse

Greater than 90° but less than 180°.

Straight

Exactly 180°.

Reflex

Greater than 180° but less than 360°.

Full Turn

Exactly 360°.

Naming an Angle

When using three letters, the vertex must be the middle letter. For example, ∠ABC has vertex B.

Common mistake: Writing the vertex as the first or last letter changes which angle is being named.

🧠 Let’s Think Together

An angle is larger than a right angle but smaller than a straight angle. What can you say about it before measuring it?

It must be an obtuse angle because its size lies between 90° and 180°. Classification is about comparing the turn with known benchmark angles.

Common Mistakes

  • Calling every large-looking angle obtuse without checking whether it is greater than 180°.
  • Putting the vertex anywhere other than the middle when naming an angle with three letters.
  • Measuring from the wrong zero on a protractor.

Exam Focus

Use exact mathematical words and include the degree symbol. When naming an angle, check the vertex first. When measuring, estimate the angle type before reading the protractor scale.

Summary

  • Acute < 90°.
  • Right = 90°.
  • Obtuse is between 90° and 180°.
  • Straight = 180°, reflex is between 180° and 360°.
