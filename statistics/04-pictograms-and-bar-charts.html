STATISTICS · LESSON 04

Pictograms and Bar Charts

Read and construct clear visual displays for categorical and discrete data.

⏱️ 22–30 min 📘 Foundation 📊 Interpretation included
x̄ % Σ

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • interpret pictogram keys;
  • use partial symbols correctly;
  • construct bar charts;
  • choose suitable scales;
  • identify misleading visual choices.

Introduction

This lesson develops Pictograms and Bar Charts from meaning and patterns before moving to calculations. Look for what the quantities, shapes or symbols represent, then connect that idea to the method.

Key Notes

Read the definitions and key facts below carefully. Each rule is most useful when you can explain why it fits the situation, not simply recall it.

Pictograms

Always read the key before counting symbols.

If one ★ represents 4 learners, then 2½ stars represent 10 learners.

Bar Charts

⭐ Interactive Pictogram and Bar Chart

One ★ represents 2 learners. Move the sliders and compare the pictogram with the bar chart.

Worked Examples

Reason through the method

  1. Identify the known information and what must be found.
  2. Choose the definition, property or formula that connects them.
  3. Substitute carefully and show each step.
  4. Check the notation, units and whether the result is sensible.

Practice Questions

  1. One symbol represents 6 books. What do 3½ symbols represent?
  2. Why should a bar-chart scale start at zero in most simple comparisons?
  3. State two differences between a bar chart and histogram.
  4. What information must every bar chart include?

✅ Check Your Work

Show answers and working
  1. 3.5×6=21 books.
  2. A shortened scale can exaggerate small differences and mislead the reader.
  3. Bar-chart bars are separated and represent categories; histogram bars touch and represent continuous intervals.
  4. A title, labelled axes, a suitable scale and correctly sized bars.

Common Mistakes

  • Choosing a rule before identifying what the question describes.
  • Skipping working or changing notation part-way through a solution.
  • Accepting an answer without checking its size, sign or units.

Exam Focus

Show the mathematical relationship you are using, keep notation consistent and give a clear final answer. Use a quick estimate, diagram or inverse operation to check that the result is reasonable.

Summary

  • Keys control pictogram values.
  • Bar charts compare separate categories.
