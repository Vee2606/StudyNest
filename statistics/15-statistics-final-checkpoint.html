Statistics Final Checkpoint

Complete an exam-style assessment across the full Statistics topic.

Section A: Data and Representation

  1. Distinguish qualitative and quantitative data.
  2. Define population and sample.
  3. Find a class width from 15≤x<25.
  4. Find the pie-chart angle for 24 out of 80.
  5. State one difference between a bar chart and histogram.

Section B: Averages and Spread

  1. Find mean, median and mode of 4,5,5,6,10.
  2. Given Q₁=21 and Q₃=39, find IQR and semi-IQR.
  3. Find midpoint of 40≤x<60.
  4. Explain why mean may be unsuitable when there is a large outlier.

Section C: Grouped Data and Evaluation

  1. Find density when frequency=32 and width=8.
  2. For N=100, state Q₁, median and Q₃ positions.
  3. Explain why histogram area represents frequency.
  4. Identify one problem with surveying only volunteers.
  5. Explain why correlation does not prove causation.

  1. Qualitative data are categories/descriptions; quantitative data are numerical.
  2. Population: entire group; sample: selected subset.
  3. 25−15=10.
  4. 24÷80×360=108°.
  5. Bar-chart bars are separated categories; histogram bars touch for continuous intervals.
  6. Mean=30÷5=6; median=5; mode=5.
  7. IQR=39−21=18; semi-IQR=9.
  8. 50.
  9. The extreme value pulls the mean away from the typical values.
  10. 32÷8=4.
  11. Q₁=25th, median=50th, Q₃=75th.
  12. For unequal widths, area=density×width=frequency.
  13. Volunteers may differ systematically from non-volunteers, creating self-selection bias.
  14. A third factor or coincidence may explain the association; an experiment or stronger evidence is needed for causation.
