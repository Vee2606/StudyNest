Introduction and Vector Notation

Understand magnitude, direction and the language of vectors.

What Is a Vector?

A vector is a quantity with both magnitude and direction. It can be represented by an arrow whose length shows size and whose arrowhead shows direction.

A vector answers two questions: “How far?” and “In which direction?”

Vectors and Scalars

Vector quantities

Displacement, velocity, force and acceleration.

Scalar quantities

Distance, speed, mass, temperature and time.

Notation

The vector from A to B may be written as AB⃗, a in bold, or a column vector.

🧭 StudyNest: How to Solve Vector Problems

1ReadIdentify the starting point, endpoint, direction and required vector.
2RepresentDraw an arrow or write the vector in column form.
3ChooseDecide whether to add, subtract, scale or find magnitude.
4WorkOperate on horizontal and vertical components separately.
5SimplifyCollect like vector terms and reduce numerical components.
6InterpretState what the result means as a movement or displacement.
7CheckConfirm the direction and signs match the diagram.
8ConcludeGive the final vector or magnitude in correct notation.

📘 Remember

Vectors carry both magnitude and direction. Work with horizontal and vertical components separately, and keep signs consistent.

⚠️ Common Mistakes

Do not confuse a point with its position vector. For AB, always calculate B − A, not A − B.

⭐ Exam Tips

Sketch arrows, label directions clearly, show component working and check whether the final direction agrees with the diagram.

🌍 Did You Know?

Vectors are used in navigation, animation, engineering, robotics, weather modelling and computer games.
