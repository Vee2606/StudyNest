TRANSFORMATIONS · LESSON 04

Reflections in Diagonal and General Lines

Use coordinate rules and perpendicular geometry for non-axis reflections.

⏱️ 26–38 min 📘 Developing 📍 Coordinate diagrams included
T ↻ k=2

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • reflect points in y=x and y=−x;
  • reflect in general straight lines;
  • use coordinate mappings and geometry together;
  • identify invariant points.

Diagonal Reflection Rules

Reflection in y=x

(x,y)→(y,x)

Reflection in y=−x

(x,y)→(−y,−x)

Worked Examples

(2,5) reflected in y=x becomes (5,2).

(2,5) reflected in y=−x becomes (−5,−2).

General Mirror Lines

For a line such as y=2x+1, draw the line accurately and ensure each object-image segment is perpendicular to it and bisected by it.

Do not merely swap coordinates unless the mirror line is exactly y=x.

Practice

  1. Reflect (−3,6) in y=x.
  2. Reflect (4,−1) in y=−x.
  3. Which points remain fixed under reflection in y=x?

✅ Check Your Work

Show answers and working
  1. (6,−3).
  2. (1,−4).
  3. All points lying on y=x, because they are already on the mirror line.

🧠 Learn It Visually: Diagonal Reflections

Diagonal mirror lines swap the roles of horizontal and vertical movement. Use coordinate rules to avoid guessing.

In y=x

(x,y) → (y,x). Swap the coordinates.

In y=−x

(x,y) → (−y,−x). Swap and change both signs.

Visual check

The segment joining a point and image must meet the diagonal mirror line at 90° and be bisected by it.

🎛️ See the Transformation Happen

Select an option and watch the object change. Compare orientation, size and position.

Choose a transformation.
Think Like a Mathematician: Why does reflecting (3,1) in y=x produce (1,3)?

🧷 Remember

For y=x, swap coordinates. For y=−x, swap coordinates and change signs.

⚠️ Common Mistake

Using the y=x rule for y=−x and forgetting the sign changes.

🎯 Exam Tip

Test one vertex with the coordinate rule before plotting the whole image.

🧭 StudyNest: How to Solve Transformation Problems

1 Compare Look at the object and image before calculating anything.
2 Identify Decide whether the change is a slide, flip, turn, resize, stretch or shear.
3 Locate Find the vector, mirror line, centre, invariant line or centre of enlargement.
4 Measure Determine direction, angle and scale factor where needed.
5 Map Apply the coordinate rule or matrix to one vertex at a time.
6 Plot Plot every image point accurately and join them in the same order.
7 Describe Name the transformation completely—not only its type.
8 Check Verify distances, orientation, size and invariant features.
