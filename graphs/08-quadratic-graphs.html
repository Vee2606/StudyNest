What Is a Quadratic Graph?

A quadratic function contains an x² term. Its graph is called a parabola.

y = x² − 4
x−3−2−10123
y50−3−4−305
A quadratic graph is smooth and curved. Do not join its points using separate straight line segments.

Important Features

Roots

Where the graph crosses the x-axis. At these points, y = 0.

y-intercept

Where x = 0.

Turning point

The lowest or highest point of the curve.

Axis of symmetry

A vertical line dividing the parabola into matching halves.

Worked Example: Reading Roots

The graph of y = x² − 4 crosses the x-axis at x = −2 and x = 2.

1

At an x-intercept, y = 0.

2

Therefore x² − 4 = 0.

3

The graph shows the solutions x = −2 and x = 2.

Solving Equations Graphically

To solve x² − 4 = 3, find where the graph y = x² − 4 has height y = 3.

  1. Draw or imagine the horizontal line y = 3.
  2. Read the x-coordinates where it meets the parabola.
  3. Those x-values solve the equation.
Graphical solutions are usually approximate. Read values to a sensible degree of accuracy.

Practice Questions

Foundation
  1. Complete a table for y = x² − 1 from x = −3 to x = 3.
  2. Plot the points and draw a smooth curve.
  3. Read the roots from your graph.
Developing
  1. Draw y = x² − 2x − 3 for −2 ≤ x ≤ 4.
  2. Estimate the roots and turning point.
  3. Use the graph to solve x² − 2x − 3 = 2.
Challenge
  1. Explain why the graph of y = −x² + 4 opens downward.

One-Minute Summary

  • A quadratic function contains x².
  • Its graph is a smooth parabola.
  • Roots occur where y = 0.
  • Graphs can solve quadratic equations approximately.
