Reading and Plotting Coordinates

Learn the x-first rule and place points accurately in all four quadrants.

⏱️ 12–15 min 📘 Foundation ✏️ Worked examples included

🎯 By the End of This Lesson...

  • read coordinates in the correct order;
  • plot points in all four quadrants;
  • identify points lying on an axis;
  • check whether a plotted point is reasonable.

🤔 Think About This...

Two students are asked to plot the point (3, 2).

One moves 3 units up and 2 units right. The other moves 3 units right and 2 units up. Who is correct?

The second student is correct because coordinates are always written in the order x, then y.

How Do Coordinates Work?

(x, y) = (sideways movement, vertical movement)
Key idea: Move along the x-axis first, then move parallel to the y-axis.

Points on the Axes

A point lies on the x-axis when its y-coordinate is zero. A point lies on the y-axis when its x-coordinate is zero.

A(6, 0) lies on the x-axis    B(0, −4) lies on the y-axis
A zero coordinate does not mean the point is the origin. Both coordinates must be zero for the point to be the origin.

⚠️ Common Mistake: Reversing Coordinates

Plotting (2, 5) as though it were (5, 2) places the point in the wrong position. Read the first number across and the second number up or down.

Which axis contains (0, 7)?

Practice Questions

Foundation
  1. Plot A(2, 4), B(−3, 1), C(−2, −4) and D(5, −1).
  2. State the quadrant containing each point.
  3. Write one point that lies on the x-axis.
Developing
  1. A point lies 4 units left and 6 units up from the origin. Write its coordinates.
  2. Point R is on the y-axis and 5 units below the origin. Write R.
Challenge
  1. Plot four points that form a rectangle and write their coordinates.

One-Minute Summary

  • Coordinates are written (x, y).
  • Move sideways first, then vertically.
  • A zero y-coordinate places a point on the x-axis.
  • A zero x-coordinate places a point on the y-axis.
