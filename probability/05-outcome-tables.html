PROBABILITY · LESSON 05

Outcome Tables

Organise combined events and count ordered outcomes systematically.

⏱️ 25–37 min 📘 Developing 🎲 Visual models included
½ 50% P(A)
H

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • construct two-way outcome tables;
  • list combined outcomes without omission;
  • find probabilities of sums and conditions;
  • distinguish ordered outcomes.

Two Fair Dice

+ 1 2 3 4 5 6
1 2 3 4 5 6 7
2 3 4 5 6 7 8
3 4 5 6 7 8 9
4 5 6 7 8 9 10
5 6 7 8 9 10 11
6 7 8 9 10 11 12

There are 36 equally likely ordered outcomes.

Worked Example

Sum 7 appears 6 times, so P(sum 7)=6/36=1/6.

(1,6) and (6,1) are different ordered outcomes, although both give sum 7.

Practice

  1. Find P(sum 2).
  2. Find P(sum greater than 10).
  3. Find P(both dice show the same number).
  4. Why is sum 7 more likely than sum 2?

✅ Check Your Work

Show answers and working
  1. Only (1,1), so 1/36.
  2. Sums 11 or 12: (5,6),(6,5),(6,6), so 3/36=1/12.
  3. Six doubles, so 6/36=1/6.
  4. Six ordered pairs give 7, but only one pair gives 2.

🧭 StudyNest: How to Solve Probability Problems

1 Describe State the experiment, event and exact outcome required.
2 List Write the sample space or organise outcomes in a table or tree.
3 Check Decide whether outcomes are equally likely and whether replacement occurs.
4 Choose Select complement, addition, multiplication or experimental probability.
5 Calculate Count favourable outcomes or follow and combine branches.
6 Simplify Give the probability as a fraction, decimal or percentage as requested.
7 Interpret Explain what the probability says about repeated trials.
8 Check Ensure the answer lies between 0 and 1 and branch totals are correct.

Lesson Support

💡 Remember

Outcome tables organise two-stage experiments systematically.

⚠️ Common Mistakes

Ordered outcomes such as (1,2) and (2,1) may be different.

⭐ Exam Tips

Label rows and columns clearly before counting outcomes.

🌍 Did You Know?

Outcome tables are useful for games involving two dice or two spinners.
