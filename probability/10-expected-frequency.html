PROBABILITY · LESSON 10

Expected Frequency and Applications

Use probability to estimate event counts over repeated trials.

⏱️ 25–37 min 📘 Developing 🎲 Visual models included
½ 50% P(A)
H

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • calculate expected frequency;
  • estimate numbers of events in repeated trials;
  • distinguish expectation from a guaranteed result;
  • reverse expected-frequency calculations.

Expected Frequency

Expected frequency = probability × number of trials.

If P(red)=0.3 and a spinner is used 200 times, expected red frequency=0.3×200=60.

Interpret Carefully

Expected frequency is a long-run prediction. Exactly 60 reds are not guaranteed in one set of 200 spins.

Worked Reverse Example

An event is expected 45 times in 180 trials.

P(event)=45/180=1/4.

Practice

  1. P(win)=0.08. Find expected wins in 500 games.
  2. A six is expected 120 times. Approximately how many fair die rolls are planned?
  3. Why might observed frequency differ from expected frequency?

✅ Check Your Work

Show answers and working
  1. 0.08×500=40 wins.
  2. (1/6)n=120, so n=720 rolls.
  3. Random variation causes actual trials to fluctuate around expectation.

🧭 StudyNest: How to Solve Probability Problems

1 Describe State the experiment, event and exact outcome required.
2 List Write the sample space or organise outcomes in a table or tree.
3 Check Decide whether outcomes are equally likely and whether replacement occurs.
4 Choose Select complement, addition, multiplication or experimental probability.
5 Calculate Count favourable outcomes or follow and combine branches.
6 Simplify Give the probability as a fraction, decimal or percentage as requested.
7 Interpret Explain what the probability says about repeated trials.
8 Check Ensure the answer lies between 0 and 1 and branch totals are correct.

Lesson Support

💡 Remember

Expected frequency equals probability multiplied by number of trials.

⚠️ Common Mistakes

Expected frequency is a prediction, not a guarantee.

⭐ Exam Tips

Check that expected frequencies across all outcomes add to the total trials.
