PROBABILITY · LESSON 06

Mutually Exclusive and Overlapping Events

Use addition rules without double-counting shared outcomes.

⏱️ 25–37 min 📘 Developing 🎲 Visual models included
½ 50% P(A)
H

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • identify mutually exclusive events;
  • apply the addition rule;
  • distinguish “or” from “and”;
  • handle overlapping events correctly.

Introduction

This lesson develops Mutually Exclusive and Overlapping Events from meaning and patterns before moving to calculations. Look for what the quantities, shapes or symbols represent, then connect that idea to the method.

Key Notes

Read the definitions and key facts below carefully. Each rule is most useful when you can explain why it fits the situation, not simply recall it.

Mutually Exclusive Events

Two events are mutually exclusive if they cannot happen in the same trial.

For mutually exclusive A and B: P(A or B)=P(A)+P(B).

On one die roll, “roll 1” and “roll 6” are mutually exclusive.

Overlapping Events

If events can happen together, adding directly counts the overlap twice.

P(A∪B)=P(A)+P(B)−P(A∩B).

Venn Illustration

Practice Questions

  1. On a die, find P(1 or 5).
  2. Are “even” and “greater than 3” mutually exclusive?
  3. Given P(A)=0.6, P(B)=0.5, P(A∩B)=0.2, find P(A∪B).

✅ Check Your Work

Show answers and working
  1. 1/6+1/6=2/6=1/3.
  2. No. Outcomes 4 and 6 belong to both events.
  3. 0.6+0.5−0.2=0.9.

Common Mistakes

  • Choosing a rule before identifying what the question describes.
  • Skipping working or changing notation part-way through a solution.
  • Accepting an answer without checking its size, sign or units.

Exam Focus

Show the mathematical relationship you are using, keep notation consistent and give a clear final answer. Use a quick estimate, diagram or inverse operation to check that the result is reasonable.

Summary

  • Understand the meaning of each idea before selecting a method.
  • Use definitions, properties and notation consistently.
  • Show working and check that the final result is sensible.
