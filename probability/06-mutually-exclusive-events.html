PROBABILITY · LESSON 06

Mutually Exclusive and Overlapping Events

Use addition rules without double-counting shared outcomes.

⏱️ 25–37 min 📘 Developing 🎲 Visual models included
½ 50% P(A)
H

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • identify mutually exclusive events;
  • apply the addition rule;
  • distinguish “or” from “and”;
  • handle overlapping events correctly.

Mutually Exclusive Events

Two events are mutually exclusive if they cannot happen in the same trial.

For mutually exclusive A and B: P(A or B)=P(A)+P(B).

On one die roll, “roll 1” and “roll 6” are mutually exclusive.

Overlapping Events

If events can happen together, adding directly counts the overlap twice.

P(A∪B)=P(A)+P(B)−P(A∩B).

Venn Illustration

Practice

  1. On a die, find P(1 or 5).
  2. Are “even” and “greater than 3” mutually exclusive?
  3. Given P(A)=0.6, P(B)=0.5, P(A∩B)=0.2, find P(A∪B).

✅ Check Your Work

Show answers and working
  1. 1/6+1/6=2/6=1/3.
  2. No. Outcomes 4 and 6 belong to both events.
  3. 0.6+0.5−0.2=0.9.

🧭 StudyNest: How to Solve Probability Problems

1 Describe State the experiment, event and exact outcome required.
2 List Write the sample space or organise outcomes in a table or tree.
3 Check Decide whether outcomes are equally likely and whether replacement occurs.
4 Choose Select complement, addition, multiplication or experimental probability.
5 Calculate Count favourable outcomes or follow and combine branches.
6 Simplify Give the probability as a fraction, decimal or percentage as requested.
7 Interpret Explain what the probability says about repeated trials.
8 Check Ensure the answer lies between 0 and 1 and branch totals are correct.

Lesson Support

💡 Remember

Mutually exclusive events cannot occur at the same time.

⚠️ Common Mistakes

Do not add overlapping events without subtracting their overlap.

⭐ Exam Tips

Check whether the events share any outcomes before using the addition rule.
