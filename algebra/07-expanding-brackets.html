Expanding Brackets

Expanding brackets means multiplying the number or expression outside the bracket by the correct term or terms inside the bracket.

🎯 By the end of this lesson...

  • Explain what expanding brackets means.
  • Identify how many terms are inside a bracket.
  • Expand simple algebraic expressions correctly.
  • Avoid common mistakes when expanding brackets.

🤔 Think About This...

A teacher is preparing prize packs for learners. Each prize pack contains:

  • 📚 2 exercise books
  • ✏️ 1 pencil

There are 5 learners. Instead of writing the same thing five times, we can write:

5(2 books + 1 pencil)

Now multiply everything correctly.

5 × 2 books = 10 books
5 × 1 pencil = 5 pencils
5(2 books + 1 pencil) = 10 books + 5 pencils

This is exactly what we do when expanding brackets in Algebra.

📖 What Does "Expand" Mean?

To expand brackets means multiplying the number or expression outside the bracket by the correct term or terms inside the bracket.

Expanding brackets is simply multiplying correctly.

🧠 The StudyNest Thinking Method

Before expanding any brackets, always ask yourself:

👉 How many terms are inside the brackets?

A term is a part of an expression separated by a plus (+) or minus (−) sign.

x + 2

There are 2 terms.

  • x
  • 2
2x

There is only 1 term because there is no plus or minus sign.

Do not count numbers and letters separately. 2x is one term.

📝 Worked Examples

Example 1

Expand:

3(x + 2)

🔍 StudyNest Question: How many terms are inside the brackets?

There are 2 terms.

  • x
  • 2

Multiply each term once.

3 × x = 3x
3 × 2 = 6
3(x + 2) = 3x + 6

Example 2

Expand:

5(2x)

🔍 StudyNest Question: How many terms are inside the brackets?

Only one term.

2x

Multiply the whole term.

5 × 2x = 10x
5(2x) = 10x
Do NOT do this:

5 × 2 = 10
5 × x = 5x ❌

Example 3

Expand:

2(3x + 5)

🔍 StudyNest Question: How many terms are inside the brackets?

There are 2 terms.

  • 3x
  • 5

Multiply each term once.

2 × 3x = 6x
2 × 5 = 10
2(3x + 5) = 6x + 10

Example 4

Expand:

7(y − 3)

Multiply the 7 by both terms.

7 × y = 7y
7 × (−3) = −21
7(y − 3) = 7y − 21

⭐ Expanding Two Brackets: The FOIL Method

So far, we have expanded expressions with one bracket.

We can also multiply two brackets, such as:

(x + 2)(x + 5)

Each term in the first bracket must multiply each term in the second bracket.

FOIL is a shortcut that helps us remember all four multiplications:

F – First
O – Outside
I – Inside
L – Last

Example: Expand (x + 2)(x + 5)

F
First terms

x × x = x²

O
Outside terms

x × 5 = 5x

I
Inside terms

2 × x = 2x

L
Last terms

2 × 5 = 10

Now write all four products:

x² + 5x + 2x + 10

Collect the like terms:

x² + 7x + 10
(x + 2)(x + 5) = x² + 7x + 10
Important:

FOIL is only a memory shortcut for multiplying two brackets containing two terms each.

The real rule is still: multiply every term in one bracket by every term in the other bracket.

🌍 Real-Life Example

A school is preparing prize packs. Each pack contains:

  • 3 pens
  • 2 pencils

There are 8 prize packs.

8(3 pens + 2 pencils)

Multiply each item by 8.

24 pens + 16 pencils

Expanding brackets simply means multiplying every term in the bracket correctly.

⚠ Common Mistakes

Mistake 1

Forgetting to multiply the second term.

3(x + 5) ≠ 3x + 5 ❌
Mistake 2

Treating 2x as two separate terms.

Remember: 2x is ONE term.
Mistake 3

Forgetting that multiplying a negative number gives a negative answer.

🧠 Did You Know?

Factories, supermarkets and warehouses use this same idea every day. If one box contains 5 bottles, then 200 boxes contain:

200 × 5 bottles

Expanding brackets is simply multiplying groups.
