Changing the Subject of a Formula

Sometimes we already have the correct formula, but we need to rearrange it so that a different letter stands alone. This process is called changing the subject of a formula.

🎬 Rearrange without breaking equality

v = u + at
→
t = (v − u)/a

Undo operations in reverse order to isolate the new subject.

🤔 Think About This...

Imagine you're travelling from Harare to Bulawayo. You know:

  • 🚗 Distance travelled
  • ⏰ Time taken

But you don't know your speed.

The formula is:

Distance = Speed × Time

Instead of creating a new formula, we simply rearrange the one we already have.

That is exactly what changing the subject means.

📖 What is the Subject of a Formula?

The subject of a formula is the letter that is alone on one side of the equals sign.

A = lw

The subject is A because it stands by itself.

The subject is simply the letter the formula is currently solving for.

🧠 Did You Notice?

Many students confuse solving an equation with changing the subject.

Solving an equation finds the value of a variable.

Changing the subject rearranges the formula without finding a value.

🧠 The StudyNest Thinking Method

Before you begin, always ask:

👉 Which letter am I trying to leave alone?

Every step you perform should help isolate that letter.

📝 Worked Examples

Example 1

Make x the subject.

y = x + 5

🔍 StudyNest Question: Which letter must stand alone?

We want x by itself.

Subtract 5 from both sides.

y - 5 = x + 5 - 5
y - 5 = x

Write the subject first.

x = y - 5

Example 2

Make m the subject.

P = 4m

Divide both sides by 4.

P ÷ 4 = 4m ÷ 4
m = P/4
m = P/4

Example 3

Make b the subject.

a = b - 9

Add 9 to both sides.

a + 9 = b
b = a + 9

Example 4

Make t the subject.

d = st

🔍 StudyNest Question: Which letter are we trying to leave on its own?

We want t by itself.

Since t is multiplied by s, divide both sides by s.

d ÷ s = st ÷ s
t = d/s
t = d/s

Example 5

Make w the subject.

A = lw

Since w is multiplied by l, divide both sides by l.

A ÷ l = lw ÷ l
w = A/l
w = A/l

🌍 Real-Life Example

The formula for the area of a rectangle is:

A = lw

If the area is 24 cm² and the width is 6 cm, find the length.

First rearrange the formula.

l = A/w

Now substitute the values.

l = 24/6
l = 4 cm
The length of the rectangle is 4 cm.

⚠ Common Mistakes

Mistake 1

Forgetting which letter should become the subject.

Mistake 2

Doing different operations on each side of the formula. Remember, whatever you do to one side, you must also do to the other.

Mistake 3

Stopping before the required letter is completely by itself.

Mistake 4

Leaving the new subject on the right-hand side. It is good mathematical practice to write the subject on the left.
