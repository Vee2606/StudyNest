Key Notes

Read the definitions and key facts below carefully. Each rule is most useful when you can explain why it fits the situation, not simply recall it.

What Is a Matrix?

A matrix is a rectangular arrangement of numbers or quantities written in rows and columns. Each value occupies a fixed position called an entry.

Order is rows × columns—not columns × rows.

Rows, Columns and Order

A= 2 5 −1 4 0 7

A has 2 rows and 3 columns, so its order is 2×3.

Rows

Horizontal arrangements. Row 1 is 2, 5, −1.

Columns

Vertical arrangements. Column 2 is 5, 0.

Entry Notation

aᵢⱼ means row i, column j. Here a₂₃=7.

📘 Remember

Key idea

The order of a matrix is always rows × columns. Entry aᵢⱼ is found in row i and column j.

⚠️ Common Mistakes

Do not reverse the order. A matrix with 2 rows and 3 columns is 2×3, not 3×2.

⭐ Exam Tips

Before doing any operation, write the order of every matrix. This quickly shows whether the operation is allowed.

🌍 Did You Know?

Matrices organise information in computer graphics, spreadsheets, transport networks and data science.

Common Mistakes

  • Choosing a rule before identifying what the question describes.
  • Skipping working or changing notation part-way through a solution.
  • Accepting an answer without checking its size, sign or units.

Exam Focus

Show the mathematical relationship you are using, keep notation consistent and give a clear final answer. Use a quick estimate, diagram or inverse operation to check that the result is reasonable.

Summary

  • Understand the meaning of each idea before selecting a method.
  • Use definitions, properties and notation consistently.
