MEASURES & MENSURATION · LESSON 12

Frustums and Advanced Mensuration

Use subtraction and similarity to solve advanced cone and pyramid problems.

⏱️ 24–30 min 📘 Challenge
r
cm²

🎯 By the End of This Lesson...

  • understand how a frustum is formed;
  • calculate frustum volume by subtraction;
  • use similarity to find missing dimensions;
  • solve cone and pyramid frustum problems;
  • combine several mensuration ideas in one problem.

What Is a Frustum?

A frustum is formed when the top of a cone or pyramid is cut off by a plane parallel to the base.

Frustum Volume Method

Frustum volume = Large solid volume − Small removed solid volume

Worked Example

A cone has radius 9 cm and height 15 cm. A smaller similar cone of radius 3 cm and height 5 cm is removed.

1

Large volume=⅓π(9²)(15)=405π.

2

Small volume=⅓π(3²)(5)=15π.

3

Frustum volume=390π cm³.

Using Similarity

If the small and large cones are similar, their corresponding lengths share one scale factor.

Large radius 12 cm, small radius 4 cm, large height 18 cm.

Linear factor small:large = 1:3, so small height = 6 cm.

Practice Questions

Foundation
  1. A large cone has volume 500π cm³ and the removed cone has volume 32π cm³. Find frustum volume.
  2. A pyramid frustum is formed by removing a 40 cm³ pyramid from a 220 cm³ pyramid. Find remaining volume.
Developing
  1. A cone has radius 8 cm, height 12 cm. A similar cone of radius 4 cm is removed. Find the small height and frustum volume.
  2. A square pyramid has base side 12 cm and height 18 cm. A similar top pyramid with base side 4 cm is removed. Find frustum volume.
Challenge
  1. A frustum comes from a cone of radius 10 cm and height 24 cm. The top radius is 6 cm. Find frustum height and volume.

  5. Small:large radius=6:10=3:5, so small height=14.4 cm. Frustum height=9.6 cm. Volume=800π−172.8π=627.2π cm³.

One-Minute Summary

  • A frustum is a large solid with a similar smaller top removed.
  • Use similarity to find missing lengths.
  • Subtract small volume from large volume.
