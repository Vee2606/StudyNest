Density

Connect mass and volume through a practical measure of how tightly matter is packed.

🎯 By the End of This Lesson...

  • understand density as mass per unit volume;
  • use density = mass ÷ volume;
  • rearrange the density formula;
  • convert units correctly;
  • solve practical problems involving mass, volume and density.

What Is Density?

Density tells us how much mass is packed into each unit of volume.

Key idea: Two objects can be the same size but have different masses because their densities differ.
Density = Mass ÷ Volume
ρ = m / V
m = ρV
V = m / ρ

Visual Idea

Worked Example

A metal block has mass 540 g and volume 60 cm³. Find its density.

1

ρ = m ÷ V.

2

ρ = 540 ÷ 60 = 9 g/cm³.

Unit Awareness

Mass and volume units must match the required density unit. For example, kg with m³ gives kg/m³.

Practice Questions

Foundation
  1. Find density if mass is 360 g and volume is 120 cm³.
  2. Find mass if density is 7.8 g/cm³ and volume is 25 cm³.
  3. Find volume if mass is 480 kg and density is 2400 kg/m³.
Developing
  1. A cuboid 10 cm by 5 cm by 4 cm has mass 1.6 kg. Find density in g/cm³.
  2. A liquid has density 0.8 g/cm³. Find mass of 2.5 litres.
Challenge
  1. A solid cylinder has radius 3 cm, height 10 cm and mass 848.2 g. Find its density using π≈3.142.

One-Minute Summary

  • Density is mass per unit volume.
  • ρ=m/V.
  • Keep units consistent.
  • Higher density means more mass packed into the same volume.
