MEASURES & MENSURATION · LESSON 10

Similarity and Scale

Use linear, area and volume scale factors in shapes, solids and drawings.

⏱️ 22–28 min 📘 Developing
r

🎯 By the End of This Lesson...

  • identify similar figures;
  • use linear scale factors;
  • calculate area and volume scale factors;
  • use map and drawing scales;
  • solve missing-length, area and volume problems.

Similarity

Similar shapes have the same shape but may have different sizes. Corresponding lengths are in the same ratio.

Key idea: If the linear scale factor is k, then the area scale factor is k² and the volume scale factor is k³.
Length factor = k
Area factor = k²
Volume factor = k³

Clean Scale Diagram

Worked Example: Lengths

Two similar shapes have scale factor 3. A side on the smaller shape is 7 cm.

Corresponding larger side = 7×3 = 21 cm.

Worked Example: Areas

Linear scale factor is 4. Smaller area is 18 cm².

Area factor = 4²=16, so larger area = 18×16=288 cm².

Worked Example: Volumes

Linear scale factor is 2.5. Smaller volume is 32 cm³.

Volume factor=2.5³=15.625, so larger volume=500 cm³.

Scale Drawings

A scale such as 1:50 means 1 unit on the drawing represents 50 of the same units in reality.

A wall measures 8 cm on a 1:50 plan.

Real length=8×50=400 cm=4 m.

Practice Questions

Foundation
  1. Linear scale factor is 5. A smaller side is 6 cm. Find the larger side.
  2. Linear factor is 3. Find the area factor.
  3. Linear factor is 4. Find the volume factor.
Developing
  1. Two similar figures have areas 25 cm² and 225 cm². Find the linear scale factor.
  2. Two similar solids have volume ratio 64:125. Find the linear ratio.
  3. A plan uses scale 1:200. A room measures 4.5 cm by 3 cm on the plan. Find actual dimensions in metres.
Challenge
  1. Two similar cones have radii 4 cm and 10 cm. The smaller volume is 96π cm³. Find the larger volume.

One-Minute Summary

  • Similar shapes have equal corresponding angles and proportional sides.
  • Length factor k gives area factor k² and volume factor k³.
  • Scale drawings must use consistent units.
