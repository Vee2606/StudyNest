TRIGONOMETRY · LESSON 06

Bearings with Trigonometry

Use right triangles to analyse distances and directions.

⏱️ 25–35 min 📘 Developing 📐 Diagrams included
sin cos tan

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • draw three-figure bearing diagrams;
  • form right triangles from navigation information;
  • use north lines consistently;
  • calculate distances and angles in bearing problems.

Setting Up the Diagram

Worked Example

A boat travels 12 km on a bearing of 060°. Its eastward component is opposite the 60° angle.

Eastward distance=12sin60°≈10.4 km.

Northward distance=12cos60°=6 km.

Practice

  1. A plane travels 20 km on bearing 030°. Find its northward component.
  2. For the same journey, find its eastward component.
  3. Why must a north line be drawn at each relevant point?
  4. Find the reverse bearing of 075°.

✅ Check Your Work

Show answers and working
  1. 20cos30°≈17.3 km.
  2. 20sin30°=10 km.
  3. Bearings are measured from north at the point where the bearing begins.
  4. 255°.

📚 Lesson Wrap-Up

📘 Remember

  • Label the triangle before choosing a formula.
  • Use degree mode unless the question says otherwise.
  • Keep full calculator values until the final rounding step.

⚠️ Common Mistakes

  • Using the wrong reference angle.
  • Rounding too early.
  • Forgetting units or the degree symbol.

⭐ Exam Tips

  • Write the formula before substituting.
  • Sketch and label any diagram that is not already provided.
  • Check whether the answer is sensible for the shape.

🌍 Did You Know?

Surveyors, engineers, pilots and architects use trigonometry to calculate distances that are difficult or unsafe to measure directly.
