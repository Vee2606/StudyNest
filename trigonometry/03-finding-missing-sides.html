TRIGONOMETRY · LESSON 03

Finding Missing Sides

Use trigonometric ratios to calculate unknown side lengths.

⏱️ 25–35 min 📘 Developing 📐 Diagrams included
sin cos tan

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • find missing opposite, adjacent and hypotenuse lengths;
  • rearrange trigonometric equations;
  • use degree mode correctly;
  • round answers appropriately;
  • check that the hypotenuse is longest.

Worked Example 1: Find the Opposite

sin 35° = x ÷ 12

x = 12 sin 35°

x = 6.882...

x ≈ 6.88 cm

Worked Example 2: Find the Hypotenuse

Adjacent=8 cm and θ=42°.

cos 42° = 8 ÷ h

h cos 42° = 8

h = 8 ÷ cos 42°

h ≈ 10.8 cm

Practice

  1. θ=30°, hypotenuse=10 cm. Find opposite.
  2. θ=60°, hypotenuse=14 cm. Find adjacent.
  3. θ=40°, adjacent=9 cm. Find opposite.
  4. θ=25°, opposite=7 cm. Find hypotenuse.

✅ Check Your Work

Show answers and working
  1. sin30°=x/10, so x=10sin30°=5 cm.
  2. cos60°=x/14, so x=14cos60°=7 cm.
  3. tan40°=x/9, so x=9tan40°≈7.55 cm.
  4. sin25°=7/h, so h=7/sin25°≈16.6 cm.

📚 Lesson Wrap-Up

📘 Remember

  • Label the triangle before choosing a formula.
  • Use degree mode unless the question says otherwise.
  • Keep full calculator values until the final rounding step.

⚠️ Common Mistakes

  • Using the wrong reference angle.
  • Rounding too early.
  • Forgetting units or the degree symbol.

⭐ Exam Tips

  • Write the formula before substituting.
  • Sketch and label any diagram that is not already provided.
  • Check whether the answer is sensible for the shape.

🌍 Did You Know?

Surveyors, engineers, pilots and architects use trigonometry to calculate distances that are difficult or unsafe to measure directly.
