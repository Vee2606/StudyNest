TRIGONOMETRY · LESSON 05

Pythagoras or Trigonometry?

Learn how to select the correct method before calculating.

⏱️ 25–35 min 📘 Developing 📐 Diagrams included
sin cos tan

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • derive and use Pythagoras’ theorem;
  • distinguish side-only and angle-based problems;
  • choose between Pythagoras and trigonometry;
  • combine both methods in multi-step problems.

Pythagoras’ Theorem

For a right triangle: a²+b²=c², where c is the hypotenuse.

Decision Flow

Is the triangle right-angled?
↓
Are you using only side lengths?
↓
Yes → Pythagoras
No, an acute angle and side are involved → Trigonometry

Worked Examples

Side-only problem

Legs 6 cm and 8 cm.

c²=6²+8²=100

c=10 cm.

Angle-and-side problem

Angle 40°, adjacent 8 cm, find opposite.

tan40°=x/8

x=8tan40°≈6.71 cm.

Practice

  1. Find the hypotenuse when the legs are 5 cm and 12 cm.
  2. Find the missing leg when hypotenuse=13 cm and other leg=5 cm.
  3. Which method should be used when angle=37° and one side is known?
  4. A triangle has two known sides and no acute angle. Which method should be checked first?

✅ Check Your Work

Show answers and working
  1. c²=25+144=169, so c=13 cm.
  2. x²=13²−5²=144, so x=12 cm.
  3. Trigonometry.
  4. Pythagoras, provided it is a right triangle.

🧭 StudyNest: How to Solve It

1InspectLook for the right angle, the given angle and the information already labelled.
2LabelName the relevant sides or identify the triangle information.
3IdentifyState exactly what is known and what must be found.
4ChooseSelect the relationship containing the known and unknown quantities.
5WriteWrite the formula before substituting values.
6SolveRearrange carefully and use the calculator in degree mode.
7RoundUse the accuracy requested and include units.
8CheckConfirm the side lengths and angles are reasonable.

📚 Lesson Wrap-Up

📘 Remember

  • Label the triangle before choosing a formula.
  • Use degree mode unless the question says otherwise.
  • Keep full calculator values until the final rounding step.

⚠️ Common Mistakes

  • Using the wrong reference angle.
  • Rounding too early.
  • Forgetting units or the degree symbol.

⭐ Exam Tips

  • Write the formula before substituting.
  • Sketch and label any diagram that is not already provided.
  • Check whether the answer is sensible for the shape.

🌍 Did You Know?

Surveyors, engineers, pilots and architects use trigonometry to calculate distances that are difficult or unsafe to measure directly.
