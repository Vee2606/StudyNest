Introduction to Trigonometry

Understand right triangles and label their sides correctly.

⏱️ 25–35 min 📘 Foundation 📐 Diagrams included

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • explain what trigonometry studies;
  • identify right-angled triangles;
  • name the hypotenuse, opposite and adjacent sides;
  • understand that side labels depend on the reference angle;
  • recognise real-life applications.

Introduction

This lesson develops Introduction to Trigonometry from meaning and patterns before moving to calculations. Look for what the quantities, shapes or symbols represent, then connect that idea to the method.

Key Notes

Read the definitions and key facts below carefully. Each rule is most useful when you can explain why it fits the situation, not simply recall it.

What Is Trigonometry?

Trigonometry studies relationships between the sides and angles of triangles. It allows us to calculate distances or angles that may be difficult to measure directly.

Before choosing a formula, identify the triangle and label it correctly.

Labelling a Right Triangle

The Three Side Names

Hypotenuse

The longest side. It is always opposite the right angle.

Opposite

The side directly across from the reference angle.

Adjacent

The side beside the reference angle that is not the hypotenuse.

Why the Reference Angle Matters

If you choose the other acute angle, the opposite and adjacent sides swap names. The hypotenuse does not change.

Worked Examples

Reason through the method

  1. Identify the known information and what must be found.
  2. Choose the definition, property or formula that connects them.
  3. Substitute carefully and show each step.
  4. Check the notation, units and whether the result is sensible.

Practice Questions

  1. Which side is always opposite the right angle?
  2. Can the hypotenuse also be called adjacent?
  3. What happens to opposite and adjacent when the reference angle changes?
  4. Give two real-life uses of trigonometry.

✅ Check Your Work

Show answers and working
  1. The hypotenuse.
  2. No. The hypotenuse has its own fixed name.
  3. They swap because the side across from the new angle is different.
  4. For example surveying, construction, navigation, engineering, astronomy or measuring heights.

Summary

📘 Remember

  • Label the triangle before choosing a formula.
  • Use degree mode unless the question says otherwise.
  • Keep full calculator values until the final rounding step.

⚠️ Common Mistakes

  • Using the wrong reference angle.
  • Rounding too early.
  • Forgetting units or the degree symbol.

⭐ Exam Tips

  • Write the formula before substituting.
  • Sketch and label any diagram that is not already provided.
  • Check whether the answer is sensible for the shape.

🌍 Did You Know?

Surveyors, engineers, pilots and architects use trigonometry to calculate distances that are difficult or unsafe to measure directly.

Common Mistakes

  • Choosing a rule before identifying what the question describes.
  • Skipping working or changing notation part-way through a solution.
  • Accepting an answer without checking its size, sign or units.

Exam Focus

Show the mathematical relationship you are using, keep notation consistent and give a clear final answer. Use a quick estimate, diagram or inverse operation to check that the result is reasonable.
