SETS · LESSON 08

Two-Set Problems

Turn survey information into accurate Venn-diagram regions.

⏱️ 20–25 min 📘 Confidence ✏️ Worked examples included
A B A ∩ B
A ∪ B A′

🎯 By the End of This Lesson...

You should be able to:

  • translate a two-set word problem into a Venn diagram;
  • fill the intersection before the “only” regions;
  • calculate the number in at least one set and the number in neither;
  • check that all regions add to the universal total.

🌍 Why This Matters

Two-set problems model real surveys—for example, learners who study Geography, History, both subjects or neither. A systematic method prevents double counting.

Two-Set Word Problems

Venn-diagram questions often provide totals for two groups and their overlap. The safest approach is to fill the overlap first.

Start with “both”, then find the “only” regions, and finally calculate those in neither set.

Fill a two-set problem from the overlap outward

1. OverlapPut 8 in F ∩ C.
2. F only22 − 8 = 14.
3. C only18 − 8 = 10.
4. Neither40 − (14+8+10)=8.

A Reliable Method

  1. Write the overlap first.
  2. Subtract the overlap from each set total.
  3. Add all regions inside the circles.
  4. Subtract from n(U) to find neither.
  5. Check the total.

Worked Example

In a class of 40 learners, 24 play football, 18 play netball and 9 play both.

1

Both = 9.

2

Football only = 24−9=15.

3

Netball only = 18−9=9.

4

At least one = 15+9+9=33.

5

Neither = 40−33=7.

💡 Exam Tip

Start with the intersection. Subtract it from each total to find the “only” regions, then use the universal total to find neither.

Practice Questions

Foundation
  1. Of 35 learners, 20 like tea, 17 like coffee and 8 like both. Find tea only, coffee only and neither.
  2. In a survey of 60 people, 38 use WhatsApp, 27 use Telegram and 15 use both. Find the number using at least one.
Confidence
  1. In a group of 80 students, 46 study Biology, 39 study Chemistry and 12 study neither. How many study both?
  2. In a survey, 28 people chose A only, 19 chose B only, 11 chose both and 7 chose neither. Find n(U), n(A) and n(B).
Challenge
  1. n(U)=100, n(A)=x+20, n(B)=2x and n(A∩B)=x−5. If 15 belong to neither set, find x.

✅ Check Your Work

Show practice answers
  1. Tea only 12; coffee only 9; at least one 29; neither 6.
  2. 38+27−15=50.
  3. At least one = 80−12=68.
    Both = 46+39−68=17.
  4. n(U)=65; n(A)=39; n(B)=30.
  5. At least one = 85.
    (x+20)+2x−(x−5)=85
    2x+25=85, so x=30.

One-Minute Summary

  • Fill the overlap first.
  • Subtract overlap to find “only” regions.
  • Add inside-circle regions to find at least one.
  • Subtract from U to find neither.
