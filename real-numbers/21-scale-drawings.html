Scale Drawings

Create accurate drawings from real measurements.

Form 1✏️ Worked examples🎮 Interactive learning
1 cm ↔ real life

🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

  • Explain what a scale drawing is.
  • Find a drawing length by dividing a real length by the scale value.
  • Draw straight lines to a stated scale.
  • Check that a drawing remains proportional.

🤔 Start with the Idea

A scale drawing is an accurate smaller or larger version of a real object. Every length is changed by the same scale, so the shape stays proportional.

📖 From Real Length to Drawing Length

When 1 cm represents 2 m, every 2 m in real life becomes 1 cm on the drawing.

Drawing length = real length ÷ amount represented by 1 cm

For a 10 m wall:

10 ÷ 2 = 5 cm
Use a ruler and label the scale clearly underneath the drawing.

🎮 Explore the Scale

Change the map or drawing length and the scale value. Notice how the real distance changes proportionally.

🧠 Let’s Think Together

A rectangular garden is 12 m long and 8 m wide. Draw it using 1 cm represents 2 m.

Length on drawing: 12 ÷ 2 = 6 cm.

Width on drawing: 8 ÷ 2 = 4 cm.

Draw a 6 cm by 4 cm rectangle and write the scale below it.

✏️ Practice Questions

Build confidence
  1. At 1 cm = 3 m, find the drawing length for 18 m.
  2. A room is 9 m by 6 m. Drawings use 1 cm = 1.5 m. Find both drawing dimensions.
  3. Why must every side use the same scale?
✅ Check your answers
  1. 6 cm
  2. 6 cm by 4 cm
  3. To preserve the correct proportions

⚠️ Common Mistakes

  • Using different units on the two sides of a ratio scale.
  • Multiplying when the question requires division, or dividing when it requires multiplication.
  • Leaving out the unit or using a linear unit for an area.
  • Rounding too early in a multi-step calculation.

🎯 Exam Focus

Write what the scale means before calculating. Show unit conversions clearly, keep full calculator values until the final step, and include the correct unit in the answer.

📌 One-Minute Summary

  • A scale connects a drawing or model to the corresponding real object.
  • Use multiplication when moving from a drawing measurement to a real measurement.
  • Use division when working backwards from real size to drawing size.
  • Ratio scales require the same unit on both sides.
  • Area changes by the square of the linear scale factor.
