Practical Scale Problems

Solve multi-step map and plan problems.

Form 2✏️ Worked examples🎮 Interactive learning
1 cm ↔ real life

🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

  • Select a suitable scale method.
  • Convert units within multi-step problems.
  • Calculate route lengths and plan dimensions.
  • Check whether an answer is reasonable.

🤔 Start with the Idea

Practical scale questions combine measuring, unit conversion and proportional reasoning. A clear plan prevents the units from becoming confusing.

🎮 Explore the Scale

Change the map or drawing length and the scale value. Notice how the real distance changes proportionally.

🧠 Let’s Think Together

A rectangular field measures 4.8 cm by 3.2 cm on a map with RF 1 : 5 000. Find its actual dimensions in metres.

Actual length: 4.8 × 5 000 = 24 000 cm = 240 m.

Actual width: 3.2 × 5 000 = 16 000 cm = 160 m.

Answer: 240 m by 160 m.

✏️ Practice Questions

Build confidence
  1. At RF 1 : 20 000, a path is 8.5 cm. Find the real length in kilometres.
  2. A 30 m room is drawn as 12 cm. Find the scale in the form 1 : n.
  3. A map route has lengths 2.4 cm, 5.1 cm and 3.5 cm at 1 cm = 4 km. Find the journey length.
✅ Check your answers
  1. 1.7 km
  2. 1 : 250
  3. 44 km

⚠️ Common Mistakes

  • Using different units on the two sides of a ratio scale.
  • Multiplying when the question requires division, or dividing when it requires multiplication.
  • Leaving out the unit or using a linear unit for an area.
  • Rounding too early in a multi-step calculation.

🎯 Exam Focus

Write what the scale means before calculating. Show unit conversions clearly, keep full calculator values until the final step, and include the correct unit in the answer.

📌 One-Minute Summary

  • A scale connects a drawing or model to the corresponding real object.
  • Use multiplication when moving from a drawing measurement to a real measurement.
  • Use division when working backwards from real size to drawing size.
  • Ratio scales require the same unit on both sides.
  • Area changes by the square of the linear scale factor.
