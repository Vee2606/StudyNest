Representative Fraction (RF)

Interpret and convert representative fractions.

Form 2✏️ Worked examples🎮 Interactive learning
1 cm ↔ real life

🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

  • Explain what an RF scale means.
  • Keep both sides in the same unit.
  • Convert centimetres to metres and kilometres.
  • Calculate real distances from RF scales.

🤔 Start with the Idea

A representative fraction, often shortened to RF, writes a scale as a ratio such as 1 : 50 000. It has no written unit because both sides use the same unit.

📖 Reading an RF Scale

1 : 50 000

This can mean 1 cm represents 50 000 cm, or 1 mm represents 50 000 mm. In map problems we usually measure the map in centimetres.

100 cm=1 mand100 000 cm=1 km

🎮 Try It Yourself

Change the corresponding lengths and watch the scale factor update.

🧠 Let’s Think Together

A map uses RF 1 : 250 000. Find the real distance represented by 3.2 cm.

Step 1: 3.2 × 250 000 = 800 000 cm.

Step 2: Convert centimetres to kilometres.

800 000 ÷ 100 000 = 8 km

Answer: 8 km.

✏️ Practice Questions

Build confidence
  1. At RF 1 : 100 000, what does 1 cm represent in kilometres?
  2. At RF 1 : 50 000, find the real distance for 6 cm.
  3. At RF 1 : 200 000, what map length represents 14 km?
✅ Check your answers
  1. 1 km
  2. 3 km
  3. 7 cm

⚠️ Common Mistakes

  • Using different units on the two sides of a ratio scale.
  • Multiplying when the question requires division, or dividing when it requires multiplication.
  • Leaving out the unit or using a linear unit for an area.
  • Rounding too early in a multi-step calculation.

🎯 Exam Focus

Write what the scale means before calculating. Show unit conversions clearly, keep full calculator values until the final step, and include the correct unit in the answer.

📌 One-Minute Summary

  • A scale connects a drawing or model to the corresponding real object.
  • Use multiplication when moving from a drawing measurement to a real measurement.
  • Use division when working backwards from real size to drawing size.
  • Ratio scales require the same unit on both sides.
  • Area changes by the square of the linear scale factor.
