Distance and Area Using Scales

Use map scales to calculate distances and areas.

Form 3✏️ Worked examples🎮 Interactive learning
1 cm ↔ real life

🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

  • Calculate actual lengths from scale drawings.
  • Calculate actual areas using squared conversion factors.
  • Keep linear and square units separate.
  • Solve land-area and floor-plan problems.

🤔 Start with the Idea

Finding an area from a map requires more than converting one length. Since area is two-dimensional, the scale conversion must affect both dimensions.

📖 Area from a Map

If 1 cm represents 50 m, then:

1 cm² represents 50 m × 50 m = 2 500 m²

This is the area factor for the map.

Do not say 1 cm² represents 50 m². The conversion factor must be squared.

🎮 Explore the Scale

Change the map or drawing length and the scale value. Notice how the real distance changes proportionally.

🧠 Let’s Think Together

A rectangular plot is 6 cm by 4 cm on a plan with scale 1 cm represents 20 m.

Real dimensions: 6 × 20 = 120 m and 4 × 20 = 80 m.

Area = 120 × 80 = 9 600 m²

Answer: 9 600 m².

✏️ Practice Questions

Build confidence
  1. A rectangle is 3 cm by 5 cm at 1 cm = 10 m. Find the real area.
  2. At RF 1 : 2 000, a square has map side 4 cm. Find its actual area in m².
  3. A field has map area 12 cm² at 1 cm = 25 m. Find its real area.
✅ Check your answers
  1. 1 500 m²
  2. 6 400 m²
  3. 7 500 m²

⚠️ Common Mistakes

  • Using different units on the two sides of a ratio scale.
  • Multiplying when the question requires division, or dividing when it requires multiplication.
  • Leaving out the unit or using a linear unit for an area.
  • Rounding too early in a multi-step calculation.

🎯 Exam Focus

Write what the scale means before calculating. Show unit conversions clearly, keep full calculator values until the final step, and include the correct unit in the answer.

📌 One-Minute Summary

  • A scale connects a drawing or model to the corresponding real object.
  • Use multiplication when moving from a drawing measurement to a real measurement.
  • Use division when working backwards from real size to drawing size.
  • Ratio scales require the same unit on both sides.
  • Area changes by the square of the linear scale factor.
