Area Factor

Connect linear scale factor with area factor.

Form 3✏️ Worked examples🎮 Interactive learning
1 cm ↔ real life

🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

  • Explain why area changes by the square of the scale factor.
  • Calculate area factor from linear scale factor.
  • Find an image area or original area.
  • Avoid using the linear factor directly on area.

🤔 Start with the Idea

When every length is multiplied by a scale factor, both the length and width of an area change. That is why area does not change by the same factor as length.

📖 From Length to Area

area factor = (linear scale factor)²

If k = 3, then every length triples. A 1 by 1 square becomes 3 by 3, so its area becomes 9 times as large.

3² = 9
For areas, square the linear scale factor. For volumes, the corresponding rule is to cube it.

🎮 Try It Yourself

Change the corresponding lengths and watch the scale factor update.

🧠 Let’s Think Together

Two similar shapes have linear scale factor 2.5 from the smaller to the larger. The smaller area is 32 cm².

Area factor = 2.5² = 6.25.

larger area = 32 × 6.25 = 200 cm²

Answer: 200 cm².

✏️ Practice Questions

Build confidence
  1. Find the area factor when k = 4.
  2. A shape of area 18 cm² is enlarged with k = 3. Find the image area.
  3. Two similar regions have area ratio 49 : 81. Find the linear scale factor from first to second.
✅ Check your answers
  1. 16
  2. 162 cm²
  3. 9/7

⚠️ Common Mistakes

  • Using different units on the two sides of a ratio scale.
  • Multiplying when the question requires division, or dividing when it requires multiplication.
  • Leaving out the unit or using a linear unit for an area.
  • Rounding too early in a multi-step calculation.

🎯 Exam Focus

Write what the scale means before calculating. Show unit conversions clearly, keep full calculator values until the final step, and include the correct unit in the answer.

📌 One-Minute Summary

  • A scale connects a drawing or model to the corresponding real object.
  • Use multiplication when moving from a drawing measurement to a real measurement.
  • Use division when working backwards from real size to drawing size.
  • Ratio scales require the same unit on both sides.
  • Area changes by the square of the linear scale factor.
